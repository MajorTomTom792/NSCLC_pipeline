Since the 1950s, lung cancer has been the leading cause of cancer-related mortality in the US. The five-year average survival rate of lung cancer was $\sim ~27\%$, though survival rates may differ based on the stage of diagnosis and size of the cancerous mass, with significant variation depending on disease stage at diagnosis. Current detection methods might introduce patient discomfort and low sensitivity in detection of abnormal growths. However, promising approaches are being developed. In particular, exosomes, which are lipid packets containing biomolecules such as RNA and proteins, are of particular focus, not only because of the richness of their cargo but also because of their possible origin from tumerous cells. In particular, microRNAs (miRNA), a major component of exosomes, have shown particular promise as a literal snapshot of a cell, making it a promising biomarker for disease. However, current approaches to exo-miRNA transcriptomic data are not well standarized and are much limited to family-based quantification and differential expression, with comparatively low canonical+isomiR/variant based detection, which might lose possible signals for such variant-based changes that might be reflective of NSCLC cancer. In this study, we will apply a canonical and variant-sensitive miRNA-seq pipeline on exosomal/extracellular sequences taken from control, those tested positive for NSCLC, and other cancerous diseases, to determine possible canonical/isomiRs for particular focus for disease diagnostics and an application of a predictive model for detecting such candidate biomarkers for NSCLC patients.
