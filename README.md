  Since the 1950s, lung cancer has been the leading cause of cancer-related mortality in the US. The five-year average survival rate of lung cancer was $\sim ~27\%$, though survival rates may differ based on the stage of diagnosis and size of the cancerous mass, with significant variation depending on disease stage at diagnosis. Current detection methods might introduce patient discomfort and low sensitivity in detection of abnormal growths. However, promising approaches are being developed. In particular, exosomes, which are lipid packets containing biomolecules such as RNA and proteins, are of particular focus, not only because of the richness of their cargo but also because of their possible origin from tumerous cells. In particular, microRNAs (miRNA), a major component of exosomes, have shown particular promise as a literal snapshot of a cell, making it a promising biomarker for disease. However, current approaches are not standardized and are limited mostly to group-based detection, not isomiR/variant-based detection, which might lose possible signals for detection of variant-miRome that might be reflective of a particular disease. In this study, we will be developing a general form pipeline for detection of variants for exosomal-miRNA samples, and detect candidate sequence biomarkers for NSCLC patients.
